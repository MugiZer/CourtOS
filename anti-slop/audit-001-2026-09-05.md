# CourtOS UI inspection

Date: 2026-09-05. Scope: inspection only; no application code changed.

Reading this as a courtside scoring and tournament-operations app, with a restrained tennis-broadcast identity. Preserve the existing layout, forest palette, opaque score surface, condensed score typography, court tabs, and contextual drawers. Suggested direction: ENERGY 2 / RHYTHM 2 / MOTION 1, with short event-driven court choreography as an explicit exception.

The problem is uneven readability and operational hierarchy. A new visual direction, new font family, dark theme, extra decoration, or replacement navigation is unnecessary.

## Evidence and limits

Inspected the running app and its styles/components. Captured organizer screens at 1440px, tablet at 768px, phone at 390px, and court display at 900px. Measured a 320px viewport. Inspected live scoring, blocked call, dispute and restoration drawer, offline organizer, rules, voice entry, sponsor settings, changeover, and upcoming matches. Reviewed the earlier assignment/warm-up and schedule-comparison screenshots from this session.

Screenshots are in `output/playwright/audit-*.png`. The original local demo data was restored after inspecting scenarios. The dev server briefly stopped during inspection and was restarted. This audit does not certify backend behavior, outdoor legibility, real microphone capture, all browser engines, or every possible match state. Contrast measurements below use computed foreground colors against the opaque background of the tested elements; they are not a complete accessibility scan.

Priorities follow the requested skill: HIGH for hard-gate defects, MEDIUM for purpose/visual hierarchy issues, LOW for quality refinements. The suggested implementation order at the end also considers workflow impact. Recommendations are not all independent defects or prerequisites for using the local demo.

## Numbered findings

1. **HIGH | Fix the remaining text contrast failures.** Rules: R-25. Measured faded set-history numerals at **2.36:1**, small READY/history labels at **2.55:1**, DECIDING SET at **4.01:1**, the active tab detail at **4.44:1**, and AWARD POINT at **4.38:1**. Darken these colors while keeping their subordinate weight and size. Use at least 4.5:1 for normal text and 3:1 for qualifying large text. Audit drawer notes, placeholders, selected controls and timeline labels as well. Reason: secondary information still needs to be readable. Evidence: `src/styles.css`, `audit-desktop.png`, computed browser colors.

2. **HIGH | Increase small interactive hit areas.** Rules: R-03. At 390px, audio is **31 × 31**, expand court **29 × 29**, Match rules **90 × 36**, Court display **82 × 17**, and View history **91 × 17** CSS pixels. Provide 44px minimum targets using padding, without inflating every icon. Apply this consistently to drawer close, example chips and footer actions. Reason: courtside controls need forgiving touch targets. These are the skill's 44px requirements, not a claim that every sub-44px element automatically violates WCAG AA.

3. **HIGH | Remove the 320px horizontal overflow.** Rules: R-03. The document measures **360px wide in a 320px viewport**, matching `body { min-width: 360px; }`. Replace the hard width floor and adapt the score grid and tabs at narrow widths. Reason: the page must reflow rather than require sideways scrolling. Evidence: `src/styles.css:10` and browser width measurement.

4. **HIGH | Preserve local-save and pending-sync information on mobile.** Rules: R-03, C-4. `.decision-meta` is hidden below 850px, including the court display's Saved locally and pending-sync count. Keep these as a short persistent status beside the score or immediately beneath it. Reason: the phone must not hide the information that tells a scorer their offline work is retained. Evidence: `src/styles.css:397` and `DecisionBar` in `src/components/CourtView.tsx`. This is source-verified; a mobile offline court-display capture was not completed.

5. **HIGH | Keep sponsor settings reachable on mobile.** Rules: R-03, C-4. The court-heading gear is hidden below 640px and no replacement entry appears in the mobile navigation. Provide a labeled secondary menu or include Sponsor creative in the settings drawer. Reason: responsive layouts should not remove the only visible route to a feature. Evidence: mobile screenshots and `.court-heading-actions .icon-button` media rule.

6. **MEDIUM | Move the mobile decision and scoring controls directly below the score.** Rules: R-22, C-3. At **390 × 844**, point buttons start at **y=966px**. The 252px court illustration separates the score from the decision banner and scoring controls. Keep the desktop split; on phones, put score, decision and controls together, then the court visualization. A collapsible court preview is another option. Reason: the illustration's brand value should not push the main task offscreen. Evidence: `audit-mobile-live.png` and button bounds.

7. **MEDIUM | Establish a readable type scale.** Rules: R-06. There are many 7–10px labels, including 7px mobile score-column headings, 8px lifecycle badges, timeline metadata and widely tracked uppercase copy. Use roughly 12–13px for useful metadata, 14–16px for routine text and actions, and keep condensed display type for scores and major headings. Reserve uppercase for a few short broadcast labels. Reason: preserve the sports identity without making operational information miniature. Evidence: styles and desktop/tablet/mobile captures.

8. **HIGH | Resolve score-grid overflow, name wrapping and breakpoint discontinuities.** Rules: R-03, C-4. At 390px, Morgan Dubois wraps while the other names do not. At 900px in court-display mode, both Morgan Dubois and Taylor Singh split, producing four lines and crowding the serving/receiving note against the row divider. The score row has a 403px client width but 433px scroll width; the point cell extends past the scoreboard boundary. The 851px display overrides increase names to 30px and scores to 148px while retaining a split stage. Use a deliberate abbreviated-name treatment on compact screens, adequate player-column width, and a content-driven breakpoint. Do not shrink names indiscriminately. Evidence: computed browser bounds, `audit-mobile-live.png`, `audit-scorer-900.png`, `src/styles.css:545` onward.

9. **LOW | Clarify the set-history columns.** Rules: C-3, R-20. SETS contains two historical numbers per team, while GAMES contains the current set. Add explicit Set 1 / Set 2 / Set 3 labeling or a compact grouped set-history header. Keep points dominant. Reason: a new user should not have to infer what the two numbers under SETS mean. Evidence: live scoreboards.

10. **LOW | Make team identification consistent between score and controls.** Rules: C-3. Scoreboard teams are vertically arranged; point buttons are horizontally arranged and use surnames, while the scoreboard uses full names. Keep the established buttons but reinforce the mapping with consistent team naming and a restrained shared marker or row association. Reason: reduce the chance of awarding a point to the wrong team without introducing unrelated team colors. Evidence: `audit-mobile-live.png`, `audit-blocked.png`.

11. **LOW | Reduce repeated orientation labels and header depth.** Rules: C-3. Court identity appears in the tab, page heading and scene caption, with a breadcrumb and tournament summary above. On mobile, masthead and tabs occupy 148px before the court heading begins. Tighten the mobile header and simplify redundant labels; keep court tabs visible. Reason: spend more of the initial viewport on match state and actions. Evidence: desktop and mobile live captures.

12. **LOW | Keep the score or urgent state visible inside red activity tabs.** Rules: C-3, C-4. Preserve the requested red tab and click-to-mark-seen behavior. Currently any unread event replaces the useful tab detail with New court activity, concealing the score, changeover or dispute description. Keep the event count but retain an informative second line such as 15–15 or Dispute · frozen. Use a consistent active-tab underline across both red and neutral states. Reason: unread activity and match state answer different questions. Evidence: `audit-blocked.png`, `audit-mobile-offline.png`, `src/App.tsx` court-tab rendering.

13. **LOW | Put stale-score context on the scoreboard itself.** Rules: C-3, C-5. The offline organizer shows 30–15 in the score and 30–30 in recent activity; the explanatory stale-score banner sits below the court illustration on mobile. Put Last received directly beside the score and distinguish local/pending events in the history. Show last receipt time when available. Reason: these two scores are valid in different contexts, but the current visual hierarchy makes them look contradictory. Evidence: `audit-mobile-offline.png`.

14. **LOW | Separate active rules from editing upcoming rules more clearly.** Rules: C-3, C-4. Match rules opens Upcoming match rules. The current scoreboard says No-Ad, while the drawer legitimately shows Off for upcoming matches. Rename the entry Upcoming rules or begin the drawer with a compact read-only Active match v1 summary, followed by Upcoming configuration. Label both scopes before the controls. Reason: avoid making a correct version distinction look like a settings bug. Evidence: `audit-rules.png`.

15. **MEDIUM | Reserve the lime accent for the next important action.** Rules: core Part 3, R-31. In the rules drawer, three selected segments and the publish button carry similar lime emphasis. Use a quiet selected-state fill, dark text and checkmark for segments, and reserve the stronger lime for publication. Keep the forest/lime brand. Reason: selected values are context; Apply is the next decision. Evidence: `audit-rules.png` and `audit-mobile-rules.png`.

16. **LOW | Keep drawer actions visible and distinguish editing from completion.** Rules: C-4. Rules publication and dispute restoration buttons are placed after the full form/list; long dispute history pushes the restore action down. Use a sticky action footer with a clear selected-score summary. After rule publication, show a separate receipt and avoid presenting the success-labeled button as another equivalent action. Reason: users should see what will happen and whether their work is finished. Evidence: `audit-dispute-drawer.png`, `RulesPanel`, `HistoryPanel`.

17. **LOW | Make dispute choices distinguishable beyond the score.** Rules: C-3. Six large rows all say Accepted point, and the first 40–40 choice matches the current disputed score. Mark that row Current score; identify the last prior score and include useful time/team context. Show the selected change as Current 40–40 → Restore 40–30 near the action. Do not automatically select or apply a restoration. Reason: the organizer needs evidence to choose, not just a stack of similarly styled numbers. Evidence: `audit-dispute-drawer.png`.

18. **MEDIUM | Use arrows consistently by action type.** Rules: R-08. Upcoming matches uses an external-looking arrow even though it switches an internal tab; Apply, Submit, history links and demo controls also mix arrows and checkmarks. Use external arrows for actual new-view navigation, ordinary arrows for navigation where helpful, and plain action labels for submit/apply. Reason: icons should predict behavior rather than decorate every action. Evidence: `src/App.tsx`, `src/components/Drawers.tsx` and captured screens.

19. **LOW | Make the voice drawer an efficient input surface.** Rules: C-3. A large microphone medallion, headline, current score and explanatory paragraphs place the actual field relatively low. Reduce the medallion, keep current score and listening state prominent, and bring input and submit together. Keep useful permission/error guidance; put detailed production-integration explanation in the demo information area. Reason: this is a repeated scoring action. Evidence: `audit-voice.png` and `VoicePanel`. The first screenshot captured the opening animation, so its temporary translucency is not a steady-state surface defect.

20. **LOW | Provide a composed sponsor preview before applying changes.** Rules: C-4. Settings show fields and, after upload, a generic media thumbnail, but not the actual court crop with countdown and safe text regions. Preview the real changeover composition, explain cropping, and expose fitting/positioning only if needed. Label the default artwork as a demo sponsor near the preview. Reason: avoid a creative looking correct in settings but being cropped or covered on court. Evidence: `SponsorPanel`, `.sponsor-upload { object-fit: cover; }`, `audit-sponsor.png`, `audit-changeover.png`.

21. **LOW | Give the return-to-play cue more visual authority.** Rules: C-3, R-20. The sponsor headline is much larger than the countdown, and TIME mostly changes a small label in the same strip. At the cue, make Time / Return to court the dominant message while retaining the countdown and score. Use one meaningful transition and honor reduced motion. Reason: the end of a break changes the screen's priority. Evidence: `audit-changeover.png` and `SponsorScene`; the exact TIME frame was reviewed in code, not captured in this inspection.

22. **LOW | Replace marketing-style operational headings with useful information.** Rules: C-3, R-15. Examples include The right match. The right court. No trips to the desk., One point can move the whole tournament., A moment for your sponsor., and Take CourtOS for a spin. Prefer Upcoming matches, Waiting for a court, Sponsor creative and Demo scenarios. The existing short CourtOS branding can stay. Reason: repeated prose consumes space without helping the operator choose. Evidence: `audit-upcoming.png`, drawer titles and `src/components/Upcoming.tsx`.

23. **LOW | Improve upcoming-match row density and status semantics.** Rules: C-3. On desktop three rows occupy a large area despite small text. On mobile they become tall cards with substantial gaps. Increase text legibility while reducing unused vertical space; group players and format, and show readiness separately from waiting priority. Waiting longest currently occupies the readiness column with the same green check as Ready to play. Reason: priority is not the same fact as eligibility. Evidence: `audit-upcoming.png`, `audit-mobile-upcoming.png`.

24. **LOW | Give the dependency and schedule diagrams an explicit reading order.** Rules: C-3, C-4. The mobile dependency strip wraps Final onto another line with an isolated arrow. Stack it intentionally on phones. In the schedule comparison, equally flexible match boxes can look like a proportional timeline even though their durations differ. Use a shared time scale, or clearly present them as ordered steps with time labels. Keep Assigned / Planned / Projected distinctions visible. Reason: the graphic should make timing easier to understand than the text alone. Evidence: `audit-mobile-upcoming.png`, earlier `mobile.png` and `.plan-match { flex: 1; }`.

25. **LOW | Keep post-assignment explanation compact in the active court view.** Rules: C-3. Warm-up already has a result, assignment message, activity feed and on-deck panel, followed by the full 55-vs-50 comparison. Keep a compact outcome such as Semifinal A assigned · projected finish 5 min earlier and put the expanded comparison in Upcoming matches or a disclosure. Reason: once the assignment is made, beginning the match is the next task. Evidence: earlier `mobile.png` and `src/App.tsx` schedule-comparison placement.

26. **LOW | Tighten empty-state and footer spacing.** Rules: C-3. The mobile live view is roughly 1,530px tall with an empty activity feed and one on-deck match. The activity placeholder and bottom branding use more space than their information warrants. Make the empty state one compact line tied to scoring, preserve clear section gaps, and reduce footer ornament. Reason: whitespace should group related tasks rather than extend the page. Evidence: `audit-mobile-live.png`.

27. **LOW | Keep task drawers opaque throughout their entrance.** Rules: C-4, R-19. The drawer animation begins at opacity .5, briefly layering court details through form controls before settling to an opaque surface. Animate position alone, or use a shorter restrained transition. Reason: even temporary visual interference is unnecessary in scoring and dispute forms. Evidence: `@keyframes drawer-enter` and the first rules/voice captures. The settled drawer is opaque and should remain so.

## What should remain

- Court 1 / Court 2 / Upcoming matches navigation, including the requested red unread signal.
- Desktop score-left/court-right composition and contextual drawers.
- Forest green, warm near-white and tennis-lime identity.
- Condensed score numerals, flat surfaces, restrained radii, limited elevation.
- Real tennis court imagery rather than unrelated decorative illustrations.
- Explicit local-demo boundaries, history-preserving correction flow, legal next-score choices.
- Existing reduced-motion handling in CSS and the Three.js rally rendering.

The skill does not justify swapping Geist just because it is common, adding a theme switch, removing the court visual, replacing all icons, or making every panel visually different.

## Review status

- PASS, visual identity: sport-specific palette, score typography and original court scene have a clear purpose.
- PASS, broad composition: the layout is built around scoring and court operations, with no generic statistic-card shell.
- PASS, restrained surfaces: steady-state drawers and score surfaces are opaque; no pervasive glass or glow.
- PASS, sampled keyboard behavior: Match rules opened with Enter, closed with Escape, and returned focus to its trigger.
- PASS, sampled 390px reflow: no horizontal document overflow in the measured live view.
- FAIL, text contrast: specific computed failures recorded in finding 1.
- FAIL, touch sizing: specific undersized controls recorded in finding 2.
- FAIL, 320px reflow: 360px document width recorded in finding 3.
- FAIL, 900px court-display score grid: a 403px row contains 433px of content, recorded in finding 8.
- FAIL, responsive information/access parity: pending-sync metadata and sponsor settings disappear at mobile breakpoints.
- NOT CERTIFIED, complete accessibility and release readiness: this was a design inspection, not a full accessibility or production acceptance test.

This is an audit of an existing interface, not delivery of a corrected design. No clean implementation gate is claimed.

## Suggested implementation order

First address 1–8: contrast, touch areas, narrow reflow, mobile status/access, scoring proximity, readable type and name handling. Then 12–17: event/state clarity, stale-score context, rules scope and drawer decisions. Finally refine naming, icons, sponsor presentation, scheduling and vertical rhythm. Preserve the desktop layout throughout.
